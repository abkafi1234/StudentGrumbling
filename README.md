# Study: Grumbling Behavior Among STEM Students Toward Non-STEM Courses

## Overview
This study investigates the *grumbling behavior* of STEM students when enrolled in non-STEM subjects. It applies **Confirmatory Factor Analysis (CFA)** and **Structural Equation Modeling (SEM)** to identify psychological and structural drivers of student dissatisfaction.

---

## Key Findings

The research identifies several critical factors influencing the level of complaint (grumbling) among STEM students regarding non-STEM coursework:

### Primary Driver
- **Role Ambiguity** is the strongest predictor of grumbling  
  - $\beta = 0.320$, $p < 0.001$

### Stress Link
- **Work-Home Conflict** significantly increases grumbling behavior  
  - $\beta = 0.190$, $p < 0.01$

### Protective Factor
- **Willingness** acts as a buffering variable that reduces grumbling  
  - $\beta = -0.145$, $p < 0.05$

### Model Performance
- The structural model explains **42.9%** of the variance in *General Grumbling*
- Additional explained variance:
  - **Work-Home Conflict:** 24.1%
  - **Long-Term Adoption:** 20.8%

---

## Methodology

### Data Collection
- Sample size: **212 valid responses**

### Measurement Quality
- **Composite Reliability (CR):** All constructs > 0.70  
- **Average Variance Extracted (AVE):** All constructs > 0.50  
- **Factor Loadings:** Mostly > 0.80, indicating strong measurement strength  
- **Multicollinearity:** VIF values < 2.0, confirming low redundancy among variables  

---

## Critical Analysis

The findings suggest that *mental friction*, or **Switching Cost**, between STEM and non-STEM contexts is a central source of dissatisfaction. This cost indirectly increases grumbling by:

- Increasing perceived workload
- Heightening role ambiguity

Conversely, perceiving a **Switching Benefit** (e.g., soft skill development) enhances willingness to engage. Notably, willingness appears to be more effective in reducing grumbling than emphasizing long-term career advantages alone.
